# About the series

[← Back to overview](../README.md)

## Why

This concerns modern agentic AI: systems that plan a task, carry it out over many steps and check their own work, rather than assistants you ask for a piece of text. Capability keeps moving, and both opportunities and concerns follow from it.

What is worth exchanging is not mainly prompts and tips but process:

- what can be handed over at all;
- how to set a task up so that handing it over works;
- which parts of the scientific process get dramatically faster, and which classic parts of data science research become "AI food";
- which projects, or changes to running projects, become worth doing that were not before.

There is no manual, and nothing is settled. What exists is scattered individual experience, and it spreads best through sustained conversation among people with enough shared context to read each other's experience.

## The format

The group meets every other Friday. Meetings typically take one of two forms:

- **Exchange.** Members bring challenges, open questions or solutions they have found, and work through them together, including how they have restructured a piece of work around what an agent can now do.
- **Topical sessions.** A topic or question is chosen in advance. One or two people give a short introduction from their own work, and the rest of the meeting is discussion. Sometimes everyone reads up on something or tries it out beforehand, and the meeting compares what they found.

Neither form needs a curriculum. What the group covers is decided by the insights and preferences of its members.

## The level

Everyone is welcome, and no prerequisite knowledge is required. The discussion is mainly pitched at people who already have a fair amount of hands-on experience with agentic AI, though: the emphasis is on experience sharing that is useful to experienced users, not on teaching the basics.

## Stability is the point

The group is meant to be **stable**: the same people, over time. This is what makes the exchange efficient. Everyone has been present for the same material, so the group can refer back to what has been covered without re-establishing it, and members learn how each other work. That matters especially for process questions, where judging whether someone else's way of running a project would transfer to your own requires knowing how they work.

So we ask participants to *intend* to come regularly, while understanding that it will not always work out. You are welcome to come a couple of times to get an impression before deciding whether to be part of the group.

## Growing as a network of groups

The invitation is open to everyone. If the group grows too large for a real discussion, or if some people want a different time, format or a fully virtual meeting, the plan is to **form additional groups rather than enlarge one**. Each group then runs on the same terms: stable, self-organised and regular.

The groups together form a network. Across it we will hold occasional joint meetings where groups present what they have been working on and compare experiences. Different groups will go down different paths and reach different conclusions on the same questions, and those differences are worth surfacing.

People who would prefer to take part virtually are encouraged to form a virtual study group of their own, which can join the physical group for joint meetings.
